# World and campaign design

::: info Working campaign document
This is the current narrative direction for **Under the Canopy**, recorded from campaign brainstorming. It describes intended story and gameplay, not shipped missions. Skirmish is the playable mode today. Agreed cornerstones are distinguished from possibilities and unresolved decisions below.
:::

## The intended experience

A large, tragic and ultimately hopeful saga at insect scale, carried by playable heroes whom players come to know through battles, relationships and consequential decisions. The campaign is intended to be a central selling point of the game.

Classic hero-driven RTS campaigns are a reference for the prominence of heroes and the connection between story and missions. Our characters, civilizations, conflict and mythology must develop their own identity. The forest's corruption serves the epic story; an environmental message is not the creative objective.

Each mission advances the overarching story through a base-building military operation or a hero-focused adventure. Missions can establish characters and consequences, but the campaign should keep moving toward major events. Mission counts follow the story rather than determine it. Around 36 missions, roughly 12 per faction, was an initial scope reference; longer campaigns remain possible. Three substantial acts or more numerous shorter acts are both open structures.

## Agreed cornerstones

- Three launch factions: Ants, Beetles and Bees. These are development descriptors; civilization names and campaign titles remain to be invented. Termites belong to an earlier concept and are not part of this direction.
- The saga moves from Ant ambition and catastrophe, through Beetle suffering and resistance, to the Bees' deeper knowledge and a coalition finale.
- The Ant Queen and her Guard anchor the opening story. The Queen is a tragic central character whose significance extends across the saga.
- The Queen dies during the Ant campaign. Her death immediately destabilizes the colony, driving the Guard into desperation.
- The Guard pursues resurrection. Successfully bringing her back causes an immediate, deadly catastrophe: she becomes the carrier or manifestation of an ancient evil.
- The Queen is distinct from the ultimate antagonist. Her possessed form carries doom into the forest, while the power behind her needs substantial presence and development of its own.
- All three factions eventually fight together against the common enemy.
- The Queen's brief return to agency during that confrontation is a central building block: she helps the assembled armies confront the evil. The exact trigger, ability and her ultimate fate remain open.

## Factions and dramatic roles

### The Ant civilization

The dominant force at the beginning, driven by ambition and expansion. Its approachable opening roster emphasizes numerous units with specialties rather than individually overwhelming troops.

The Queen holds the civilization together politically and emotionally. The player's viewpoint belongs to her Guard: potentially one to three heroes close to her, carrying out her commands. **The Queen's Guard** is a working act or campaign title.

Her death has immediate consequences beyond anxiety about future generations. Rival Ant factions can form, military authority can fracture, and external rivals can exploit the sudden weakness. A mission can make this collapse tangible, then propel the Guard toward its desperate solution.

### The Beetle civilization

Durable, chunky units and advanced technology. Natural chitin inspires a steel-armored appearance.

Their campaign follows the Ant catastrophe and bears the brunt of the spreading corruption. Suffering is its emotional starting point; the specific heroes, achievements and path from survival to resistance remain to be developed.

### The Bee civilization

A protector order with its own agenda and the greatest understanding of the ancient threat. Its visual direction can draw on the dignity and ceremony of a holy order without committing to literal holiness or moral perfection.

Bees do not permanently fly in normal gameplay. Flight-like movement, such as jumps, can be part of their faction identity. Their knowledge becomes central to resolving the crisis, and their campaign brings the factions toward the shared confrontation. Their secrets, responsibilities and internal disagreements remain open.

## Root: progression tied to the story

The intended economy has two regular resources and a third resource, provisionally **Root**. Limited access to Root permits mid-tier progression, after which regular income can sustain those units. High-tier forces require a continuing Root supply. Exact costs and implementation are outside this narrative document.

Root comes from special deposits associated with roots in the forest, using a dedicated processing structure. Whether this structure truly purifies the material or merely extracts its power is unresolved. “Corrupted root” describes the working concept; its initial in-world name need not reveal the danger.

The opening missions can teach the two-resource economy. Around the fourth mission, the Guard secures its first Root source and discovers a meaningful military advantage. This timing is provisional. Additional deposits appear as the campaign progresses, inviting experimentation and competition.

Root should be integral to the Ant story: a source of strength during expansion and a possible basis for believing the Queen can be restored. The exact relationship between harvesting, resurrection and the ancient evil has not been chosen. Possible mechanisms include carrying the entity, weakening its containment, or enabling contact with it.

The resource also supports environmental storytelling. Early manifestations can be subtle. Later regions become visibly horrific as corruption changes roots, terrain and living creatures. Huge centipedes or other threatening forest inhabitants are possible encounters, not a settled enemy roster.

## The Ant campaign's working arc

### Ambition and discovery

Establish the Queen, the Guard and a civilization accustomed to power. Expansion and rivalry provide immediate military goals. Root enters the story as a valuable discovery, and the Ants begin to experiment with it.

### Loss and desperation

The Queen dies off-screen around the middle of the campaign in the current outline, while the Guard is away. She stays within the hive. The Guard returns to find the main colony devastated or in turmoil and discovers her death through its aftermath. Her death is certain; the cause and sequence of events remain hidden from the player. Her death is intended to be orchestrated by the overarching power, but the method and the degree of direct involvement remain unresolved.

The resulting fragmentation makes the Guard's desperation concrete. Their search for resurrection becomes the main forward drive. The path must emerge from discoveries and beliefs established earlier, rather than a new solution appearing only when needed.

### Resurrection and catastrophe

The final Ant mission can be an assault on an ancient source of power defended by Bees, dedicated guardians, or the forest itself. Defeating those defenders and securing the resurrection site gives the player a clear RTS objective.

A vast ruined altar, an opening beneath a tree, or the hollow interior of an enormous corrupted trunk are possible settings. Insect scale should make familiar natural forms feel monumental.

The player completes the resurrection and receives a brief moment of triumph. The Ants are overjoyed. Then the cinematic reveals the consequence: the returned Queen manifests the ancient evil, slaughtering those around her, taking control of the army, or both. The exact victims, survivors and sequence are undecided.

The consequences are immediate. Roots begin visibly corrupting the surroundings, the Ants fall into disarray, and the threat passes directly into the Beetle campaign. There is no prolonged period of apparent safety after resurrection.

## The antagonist across the saga

The antagonist needs enough time to become a main character in the conflict. Its presence should develop before the resurrection, then continue through the later campaigns. Its intentions, interventions and relationships with the heroes matter as much as the scale of its final manifestation.

The Queen is its harbinger or vessel, not automatically its true identity. The entity's nature, name, motives, history and limitations remain open. Earlier ideas about an ancient fungal consciousness or an old pact are possibilities, not established canon.

## The coalition and the Queen's final intervention

The final campaign brings all three armies together. Allied Ant and Beetle forces can already participate in earlier missions, allowing cooperation to develop before the finale.

The central cinematic possibility is that the armies reach the Queen and she sees them fighting together. Overwhelmed by what she witnesses, she briefly breaks the entity's hold and helps the entire assembled force confront it.

She might empower the armies, transport them to the true enemy, or create another decisive opening. Whether she only then understands her possession, or has understood it while unable to resist, is unresolved. Earlier glimpses of her resistance are a possible way to prepare this moment.

The final battle can have multiple phases: reaching or freeing the Queen, then confronting the ancient evil as an enormous manifested monstrosity. The mission structure remains open.

The intended emotional connection is that the Guard's attempt to save her unleashed the catastrophe, and her own final choice helps them face it. Whether she survives, dies, or pays another price is not yet decided.

## Questions to develop next

- Who are the Guard's heroes, and how does each relate to the Queen before and after her death?
- What makes the Queen loved, and how does her ambition affect those outside her civilization?
- What does Root demonstrably do that makes resurrection believable?
- How is the Queen killed, and why does that serve the antagonist's purpose?
- Why do the Guard believe they should overcome the resurrection site's defenders despite their warnings?
- Who survives the resurrection, and how do those characters connect the faction campaigns?
- What does the ancient entity want, and how does it interact with the heroes before gaining a body?
- What indispensable victories do the Beetles and Bees achieve on the road to the coalition?
- What becomes of Root as a usable resource once its danger is understood?
- What does the Queen's final intervention cost her?
